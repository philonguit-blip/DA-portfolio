IMPORTANT REPRODUCTION FINDINGS
1. The original EDA notebook is not top-to-bottom reproducible without restoring hidden interactive state:
   several df_* aliases are used before being created; some variables used by the marketing section
   (orders_detail and bounce_rate_web_traffic) are absent from the saved notebook.
2. The original channel-AOV formula divides RPS by CR expressed as a percentage, producing AOV values
   that are 100x too small. Correct channel AOV is revenue / orders.
3. Source-level channel CR is not portfolio-safe because web_traffic has one row per date with one
   traffic_source, while orders stores order_source per order. The denominator is therefore not cleanly aligned
   by channel. The portfolio replacement uses overall time-aligned conversion instead.
4. The promotion dashboard contains hard-coded summary DataFrames. The replacement charts are calculated
   directly from raw data.
5. The inventory dashboard mixes full-history and 2020–2022 metrics in its narrative. The replacement chart
   uses one consistent 2020–2022 period.
6. The final model notebook is reproducible after replacing the Kaggle path with repository-local paths.
   Its saved validation WAPE is 0.342556. The report mentions lag/rolling features and SHAP, but the final
   model.ipynb feature list does not contain lag/rolling features and contains no SHAP analysis.
7. baseline.ipynb is not reproducible as a complete notebook: it contains duplicated experimental blocks,
   undefined configuration variables, and an empty-test construction that cascades into downstream errors.

8. The original inventory narrative says replenishment spikes were not tracking sales. Reproduction does not support that claim:
   monthly units_received and units_sold in 2020–2022 are almost perfectly correlated (r=0.9999),
   while receipts average 14.7% above sales. A safer interpretation is systematic over-replenishment,
   not demand-disconnected batch ordering.
